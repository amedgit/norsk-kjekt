Norskprøve B1: what it is and how to prepare

The Norskprøve is not a grammar exam: it measures what you can actually do in Norwegian, across four independent tests. Here is the real structure, the timings and what to practise if B1 is your goal.

Take a practice testLevel test

What the Norskprøve measures

The Norskprøve A1–B2 is a standardised test of communicative competence in Norwegian. It measures levels A1, A2, B1 and B2 of the Common European Framework of Reference. It is administered by HK-dir (the Norwegian Directorate for Higher Education and Skills) and taken at test centres across the country.

The most important detail for planning your study: there is no syllabus. The test does not assess theoretical knowledge of grammar or of specific topics. That completely changes how you prepare: you do not memorise, you practise.

The four tests and their timings

They are four independent delprøver. Each skill is assessed separately and you can get a different result in each one.

The first three are done on a computer and are often grouped as the «written test». The test centre may decide the order. The oral test is taken separately, usually with another candidate at the same level.

Reading and listening: the same test for everyone

For lesing and lytting you do not register for a level. All candidates take the same test, which is partly adaptive: if you answer correctly you get more, harder questions. There are two pretests of about seven questions each, then a main test of 15 to 25 questions.

You are not told how many you answered correctly, only the final result. And you can make a fair number of mistakes and still reach B2, so do not fixate on your error count while practising.

Writing: what each level asks for

Here you do choose a level when you register, and the rule is clear: in writing you cannot get a higher level than the test you sit, but you can get a lower one. Register for A2–B1 and you can get A2; register for B1–B2 and you can get B1.

  • A1–A2 (90 min, 3 tasks): write a short message, describe a picture (50–80 words) and write about a familiar topic (80 words or more).
  • A2–B1 (90 min, 3 tasks): describe a picture (80–100 words), write about a familiar topic (80–200 words) and write an email justifying an opinion (80 words or more).
  • B1–B2 (120 min, 2 tasks): a formal text and an argumentative text defending your point of view.

Two criteria matter more than grammar: that the text is understandable and that it has thematic and logical coherence. At B1 and B2, correct vocabulary is also checked: words from other languages count as mistakes.

Speaking: what it is really like

The oral test lasts 20 to 25 minutes and is taken with two candidates at the same level, an examiner who leads the conversation and a rater who assesses. Levels A1–A2 have four tasks; A2–B1 and B1–B2 have three.

The task types repeat: introducing yourself, describing a picture, talking about an everyday topic (school, work, family, food, weather), talking with the other candidate and giving and justifying your opinion. A perfect accent is not required: dialect is allowed.

How it is assessed

Writing and speaking are assessed by external raters from across the country, who do not know your identity or your previous results. You receive a prøvebevis with a separate result for each skill, not a single grade.

That format has a practical consequence: you can have B1 in reading and A2 in writing. So it is worth knowing which skill is holding you back before you sit the test.

What the result is used for

The documented level is used, among other things, for residence and citizenship procedures, for authorisation of healthcare professions and for admission to studies. The exact requirements change and depend on your situation, so always check them at hkdir.no and udi.no.

A useful historical note: the old Norskprøve 2 and Norskprøve 3 were held for the last time in February 2014. They correspond roughly to A2 and B1 of the current test.

How to prepare in four steps

  1. Measure where you are. Take a level test and a full practice test to see which skill is weakest.
  2. Attack the weakest skill first. If it is writing, practise the tasks for the level you will register for, with their word limits.
  3. Practise with a timer. Time is part of the exam: 75 minutes of reading and 90 or 120 of writing change the difficulty a lot.
  4. Simulate the full day. Do all four tests back to back at least once to get used to the fatigue.

Common mistakes

  • Registering for a level that is too high. Especially B1–B2: if your Norwegian is not solid, it is hard to answer understandably.
  • Studying grammar instead of producing. The exam measures communication; you have to write and speak, not just do exercises.
  • Ignoring writing. It is the skill people get stuck on, and the one you can train most with feedback.
  • Using words from other languages. At B1 and B2 they count as mistakes, even if a Norwegian understands you.
  • Not reviewing your text. Saving five minutes at the end to fix your own errors raises the result.

Frequently asked questions

What level is the Norskprøve B1?

The Norskprøve measures four CEFR levels: A1, A2, B1 and B2. It is not a single B1 exam: if you register for the B1–B2 test you can get B1 or B2, and also a lower result if your performance does not reach it.

How long does the Norskprøve take?

Listening: 30–60 minutes. Reading: 75 minutes. Writing: 90 minutes at A1–A2 and A2–B1, and 120 minutes at B1–B2. Speaking: 20–25 minutes. Reading, listening and writing are done on a computer; the oral test is face to face with an examiner and a rater.

Is there a syllabus for the Norskprøve?

No. The Norskprøve does not test theoretical knowledge of grammar or of specific topics: it measures communicative competence. There is no syllabus to memorise, so you prepare by practising the four skills.

Can I get B1 if I register for the A2–B1 test?

Yes. In the writing test you cannot get a higher level than the test you registered for, but you can get a lower one. In the oral test you can get a result both lower and higher than your registration level.

Is the Norskprøve the same as the Bergenstest?

No. The Bergenstest is a different Norwegian test. The Norskprøve A1–B2 is administered by HK-dir and is the one normally used to document Norwegian level for official purposes and studies in Norway.

Information checked on 8 November 2026 against the official HK-dir pages. Rules and formats can change: always confirm the details at hkdir.no before registering.
